Call 911 firstPut the phone on speaker. Send someone for an AED.

  1. Check the scene is safe

    Make sure you are not in danger before you go closer.

  2. Check for response

    Tap their shoulders and shout "Are you OK?"

  3. Check breathing

    Look for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is not normal breathing.

  4. Start compressions

    Heel of your hand on the center of the chest, other hand on top. Push at least 2 inches deep, 100 to 120 times a minute.

  5. Use an AED as soon as it arrives

    Turn it on and follow its voice prompts.

  6. Keep going

    Do not stop until help takes over, the AED tells you to, or the person starts breathing.

CPR by age group

The basics differ a little for adults, children and infants.

Hands

Two hands, center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone).

Depth

At least 2 in (5 cm), no more than 2.4 in.

Rate

100 to 120 compressions per minute.

Breaths

30 compressions to 2 breaths, or hands-only if untrained.

Hands

One or two hands, center of the chest. Age 1 to puberty.

Depth

About 2 in (5 cm), one third of the chest.

Rate

100 to 120 compressions per minute.

Breaths

30:2 with one rescuer, 15:2 with two.

Hands

Two fingers, or two thumbs encircling, just below the nipple line. Under age 1.

Depth

About 1.5 in (4 cm), one third of the chest.

Rate

100 to 120 compressions per minute.

Breaths

30:2 with one rescuer, 15:2 with two.

How to set up

  1. Set the camera side-on, about 1.5–2 m (5–6 ft) away.
  2. Keep your shoulders, elbows and hands in view.
  3. Turn the volume up, then start compressions on a manikin or pillow, never a person.
